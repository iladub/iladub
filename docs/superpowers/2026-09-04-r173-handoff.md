## 5. The next concrete action

Two actions, typed separately, and **the ASSERTED one must be done first** — the PROPOSED one is
built on a prediction that the ASSERTED one either confirms or kills.

### 5a. ASSERTED — bisect the eight

Run the three test files at a handful of earlier commits, in a worktree with the corpus symlinked,
and record **when each of the eight started failing**. Candidate points, newest first: `4cfee38`
(2026-09-02, the suspected origin), `3055ae8`, `20cc5b8`, `63d6fdc` (2026-08-09, the last change to
`test_adoption_document.py` itself).

This is **asserted**: the outcome is unknown but the act is mechanical, doing it *is* the work, and
it cannot fail in a way that wastes the loop. It is also the cheapest thing that can kill 5b.

**It is the right first action for a reason worth stating**: R173 was raised from a single
base-vs-HEAD comparison. That establishes *"not R165's"* and nothing else — in particular it does
not establish that the eight arrived together, which is the whole of 5b's premise.

### 5b. PROPOSED — the eight are one finding, and it is R160's

**The prediction:** all eight fail for the single reason that `DocumentReport.adopted` became `()`
at `4cfee38`, an intended consequence the 2026-09-02 loop recorded in R160 and never re-baselined
in the tests that depended on the old value. If so, the loop is a re-baseline with a ruling — each
of the eight retargeted or re-baselined with what its new number MEANS — plus a decision on the CI
visibility half.

**Why it is PROPOSED and not asserted:** the stem score mismatch is the counter-evidence, and it is
in the fourth decimal of a document score, which is the signature of a READING change rather than an
adoption change. If stem turns out to have a different origin commit from the other seven, this
prediction is refuted and the loop is two findings, not one — and the plan a session would have
written from 5b would be wrong.

**If 5b is refuted, do not improvise a replacement in the same session.** Record the bisect result,
re-shape R173's row to match, and let a fresh session spec it. This repo's own record (R151, and
this loop's eight plan defects) is that the expensive failures come from building on a prediction
nobody ran.

### What NOT to do

- **Do not fold the CI-visibility half into the same change as the eight re-baselines** unless the
  bisect shows they are genuinely one thing. They are separable, and R173's row lists them as two
  paths deliberately.
- **Do not "fix" a failing test by weakening its assertion.** Several of the eight are adoption
  *epistemics* tests (`test_the_admission_verdict_names_its_agent`,
  `test_no_superseded_band_keeps_its_escalation_candidate`); a page that no longer adopts needs a
  fixture that still adopts, or an explicit deletion with a recorded reason — the ruling the R165
  loop made for `test_region_tiles_rationale_names_the_real_unit` is the worked precedent.
