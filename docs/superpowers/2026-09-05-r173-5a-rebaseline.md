two ways out for the six adoption tests apple can no longer exercise — *"a fixture that still
adopts, or an explicit deletion with a recorded reason"* — and, before choosing, this loop

1. **No tracked corpus document adopts anything.** All seven report `adopted=()`.

[[R173]]'s CI-visibility half, and it was not the plan; it fell out of measuring the fork.

is [[R172]]'s detector (bisect § 5), not a stale pin.

## 6. What is NOT done, and what is NOT claimed

- **[[R173]]'s CI-visibility half is untouched.** Four pins moving into CI's reach is a
  by-product, not the remedy: nothing here makes an unexpectedly-skipped corpus test LOUD, and
  the corpus is still gitignored. That half stays unpriced.
- **[[R172]] is not ruled.** Whether apple p1's 56 asserted cells is the right reading is exactly
  what the one deliberately-failing test is asking, and this loop does not answer it.
- **[[R160]] is not settled.** The reader-authority question still has no corpus instance; § 1
  strengthens the row (the candidate gate fires twice on bfs, the adoption branch never) without
  answering it.
- **The synthetic fixture is not apple.** It carries one band, one superseded region and no
  `ignored` region, so it cannot control for "a change that supersedes EVERY region" — the control
  test stays on apple. A fixture with a second, untouched band would be strictly better and was
  not built here.
- **No claim that the re-pointed pins are equivalent in strength to the apple ones.** They pin the
  same mechanisms, falsified individually in § 4, on a one-band document rather than a 28-row page.
- **A second, UNTOUCHED band in the synthetic fixture was not drawn**, which is why the control
  test stays on apple and corpus-gated while the five pins it controls do not. Raised as [[R175]]
  rather than left here.
